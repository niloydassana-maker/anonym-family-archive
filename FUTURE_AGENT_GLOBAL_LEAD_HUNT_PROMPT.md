## Matching and ranking

Rank each candidate by:

- Evidence of a real buyer and a specific live problem.
- Fit to demonstrated, verifiable skills (not just matching tool names).
- Budget, payment milestones, platform protection, payout delay, and whether money can realistically arrive by the user's deadline.
- Freshness and status confidence; buyer's recent activity; proposal competition and interviews.
- Territory/time overlap, written-only compatibility, access feasibility, and any mandatory technology or prior-deployment requirement.
- Risk signals: unusually large scope for tiny budget, client history, payment-on-delivery only, platform account age, incoherent or copied post, confusing date metadata, or request for free work/credentials.

Separate “best technical match,” “fastest bounded paid task,” and “high-risk/high-value option.” Never call a match “100%” or state a conversion probability. An attractive advertised budget is not proof of award or payment.

## Drafting directive

For every actionable lead, prepare a distinct draft using the buyer's own problem statement and exact requested application format. Each draft should:

1. Open with a specific reference to the buyer's actual need, not generic praise.
2. State the closest relevant work and exactly what is verifiable. Clearly distinguish an adjacent architecture reference from a completed deployment of the buyer's specific vendor/product.
3. Propose a bounded first deliverable, acceptance checks, data/access assumptions, failure handling, human approval where relevant, and handover.
4. Ask one or two useful qualification questions only when the brief lacks scope or eligibility details.
5. State price, milestone split, delivery time, or availability only as a proposed term, conditional on scope/access and clearly not buyer-agreed. Do not undercut or promise an amount without evidence and user authorization.
6. Include the LinkedIn and asset catalogue links above in every communication draft. The catalog link must not be described as public source code.
7. Observe channel norms: no email-style signature in a DM/chat; follow the destination platform's application instructions; keep payment/contact on-platform when its rules require it.
8. Do not claim direct experience with tools, client outcomes, production scale, compliance, or certifications that have not been established. If a job requests evidence the user cannot yet supply, leave a clear placeholder and flag it before submission.
