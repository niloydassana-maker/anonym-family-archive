1. Executive Summary
TransportLah is proposed as a Singapore public transport companion app that rewards greener commuting. It 
combines live public transport data, lightweight ride verification, fare-smart route comparison, Green Points, public 
leaderboards, admin-managed promotions and sponsor-backed contests.
The app should avoid competing head-on with established transport utilities on journey planning alone. Instead, it 
should use transport data as the foundation and build a differentiated engagement layer: verified rides + rewards + 
sustainability + contests.
Recommended positioning
Singapore's green commute rewards and challenge platform, powered by public transport data and privacy-first 
ride verification.
Strategic Pillar What It Means
Daily utility Live arrivals, route options, train alerts, crowd context and fare-smart 
comparisons.
Trustworthy rewards A lightweight confidence-scoring service validates rides before points 
and leaderboard credit are awarded.
Green identity Users build a visible profile around verified rides, CO2 avoided, 
streaks and achievements.
Campaign platform Admins and sponsors can create contests, promotions and public or 
organisation-specific leaderboards.
Privacy-first design Raw GPS is used only during active ride verification and retained 
temporarily.
2. Product Vision and Target Users

be built around a simple loop: Plan ride -> Verify ride -> Earn points -> Join contests -> Climb leaderboard ->

The primary tagline is: Ride green. Earn rewards. Climb the leaderboard.

Singapore already has a mature transport app ecosystem. Existing apps are strong in live bus timing, multimodal 
route planning, train alerts, fare-card account services and taxi/private-hire booking. TransportLah should therefore 
not be positioned as another generic journey planner.

3.2 Strategic Insight
 Basic bus arrivals, nearby stops, route planning, MRT alerts and favourites are table stakes.
 TransportLah should focus investment on features that existing transport apps do not strongly cover: verified 
green rides, Green Points, public leaderboards, contests, admin promotions and sustainability reporting.
 The user promise should be: Save money, ride greener, earn rewards.
Strategic conclusion
Use LTA transport data as the foundation, but build the differentiated value layer above it: verified rides + Green 
Points + contests + leaderboards + promotions + sustainability reporting.
4. LTA DataMall API Foundation
The LTA DataMall guide provides a practical foundation for live public-transport and mobility features. Relevant APIs 
include Bus Arrival, Bus Services, Bus Routes, Bus Stops, Passenger Volume, Train Service Alerts, Facilities 
Maintenance, Station Crowd Density Real Time and Forecast, Planned Bus Routes and Bicycle Parking.
Important design constraint: passenger-volume APIs are aggregated and generated for previous-month data. They 
help with analytics and planning, but they do not prove that an individual user took a specific ride. Therefore, 
TransportLah needs its own lightweight ride verification layer unless a future authorised fare-card integration is 
secured.

4.1 API Integration Principles
 Do not call LTA APIs directly from the mobile client. Use a backend proxy to protect the AccountKey.
 Cache high-volume data such as bus stops, routes and services.
 Use active-stop polling for Bus Arrival rather than constantly polling every stop.
 Handle 500-record pagination via $skip where applicable.
 Treat LTA data as transport context and validation support, not as user ride evidence.
5. Core User Features
5.1 Journey Planner
Users can search by origin and destination and compare route options. The planner should support bus, MRT/LRT 
and mixed-mode journeys, but the MVP should not try to outbuild Citymapper or Google Maps. Its priority is to 
support fare-smart, green and reward-aware route decisions.
5.2 Live Bus Stop Screen
The live bus stop screen shows services arriving soon, ETA, destination, stops away, load/crowding, wheelchair 
accessibility, vehicle type and whether arrival time is monitored from bus location or schedule-based.
Bus Load UI Treatment
SEA - Seats Available Green badge / low crowd indicator
SDA - Standing Available Amber badge / medium crowd indicator
LSD - Limited Standing Red badge / high crowd indicator
5.3 Train Status Screen
The train module shows train service status, affected lines/stations, free public bus or MRT shuttle notices during 
disruptions, real-time and forecasted station crowd levels, and lift maintenance notices.
5.4 Green Ride Check-in
The check-in experience should be voluntary and transparent. The app should say “Start Green Ride” and “We are 
verifying your ride,” rather than implying surveillance or perfect certainty.
6. Lightweight Ride Verification Service
TransportLah should use a confidence-based ride verification engine instead of continuous tracking or a single GPS 
signal. The service should classify rides as verified, probable, low-confidence or rejected based on a combination of 
stop proximity, service validity, bus arrival context, route matching, speed plausibility and alighting location.
6.1 Verification Flow
1. User taps Start Ride.
2. App captures 1-3 GPS points near a stop or station.
3. Backend checks proximity to a valid bus stop or train station.
4. User confirms service or route option.
5. Backend validates that the service serves the selected stop and that the stop is in the right sequence.
6. Backend checks live or recent Bus Arrival context where relevant.

7. During the ride, the app samples GPS lightly and preferably calculates first-pass route matching on device.
8. User taps End Ride, or the app suggests auto-ending near a downstream stop/station.
9. Backend computes verification score and awards points according to verification tier.
10. Raw GPS trace is deleted or downsampled after the retention window.
6.2 GPS Sampling Strategy
Ride Phase Sampling
Start check-in 1-3 points
First 3 minutes Every 10-15 seconds
During ride Every 30-60 seconds
Near expected alighting Every 10-15 seconds
After ride ends Stop completely
6.3 Ride Confidence Score
Signal Purpose Weight
Starts near valid bus stop Plausible boarding point 15
Selected service serves stop Route validity 10
Bus Arrival context matches check-in time Bus plausibly arrived 20
Movement follows bus route corridor Strong movement validation 25
Speed pattern is bus-like Rejects walking/cycling/implausible travel 10
Remains near route corridor Prevents drive-by check-ins 10
Exits near downstream stop Plausible alighting point 10
Score Decision Reward Impact
80-100 Verified ride Full points and leaderboard eligible
60-79 Probable ride Partial points; not high-value contest eligible
40-59 Low confidence Personal stats only
Below 40 Rejected or flagged No points
6.4 Route Matching Logic
ride_is_plausible =
  user starts near stop N
  AND selected service serves stop N
  AND user moves in the direction of stop N+1, N+2, N+3...
  AND user remains within 100-200m of the route corridor
  AND speed is within a bus-like range
  AND user exits near a downstream stop
6.5 Verification Tiers
Tier Verification Reward Impact
Tier 0 Manual ride claim Personal stats only
Tier 1 GPS near valid stop + plausible route Basic points
Tier 2 GPS + LTA live bus context + route following Full points
Tier 3 Tier 2 + QR/NFC/device integrity Contest/reward eligible
Tier 4 Future fare-card/SimplyGo partner 
verification Highest trust

7. Fare-Smart Routes
Fare-Smart Routes helps users compare routes by estimated fare, travel time, crowd level, Green Points and CO2 
avoided. In Singapore, public bus and MRT/LRT fares are distance-based, so the cheapest route is usually the 
lowest valid chargeable journey distance, subject to transfer rules.
7.1 Route Comparison Experience
Route Option Time Est. Fare Transfers Crowd Green Points
Fastest 32 min $1.95 2 Medium 25
Cheapest 41 min $1.67 1 Low 30
Least crowded 45 min $1.83 2 Low 35
Best rewards 39 min $1.83 2 Medium 45
The route card should explain trade-offs in plain language, e.g. “Save about $0.28, but the journey may take 9 
minutes longer.”
7.2 Fare Estimation Logic
 Total route distance and route sequence.
 Bus route stop distance and station origin/destination.
 Transfer limits and timing windows.
 Same-service and same-station re-entry constraints.
 Fare profile: adult, student, senior, concession or tourist.
 Monthly pass status, where relevant.
 Clear labelling as estimated unless official fare-source integration is used.
7.3 Transfer Validity Warnings
Situation App Warning
Long transfer wait This route may be charged as a new journey if the transfer wait is too 
long.
Same bus service repeated Taking the same bus service again may be treated as a new trip.
Same MRT station re-entry Exiting and re-entering the same train station may break fare 
continuity.
Too many transfers This route may exceed the maximum transfer limit.
Very long route This may be treated as a separate journey depending on total 
duration.
Fare-Smart positioning
Save money, ride greener, earn rewards.

13. Fraud, Privacy and Consent
13.1 Fraud Controls
 Location plausibility and stop geofence checks.
 Bus service and route validation.
 Speed plausibility and impossible movement detection.
 Duplicate ride and repeated short ride detection.
 Daily point caps and minimum ride distance/duration.
 GPS spoofing and device integrity checks.
 QR/NFC verification for high-value contests.
 Manual review of top contest winners and suspicious rides.
 Verified-only leaderboards for public contests.
13.2 Privacy Model
Data Retention
Raw GPS trace 24-72 hours
Flagged ride trace Until review is complete
Aggregated ride summary Long term

14. Proposed Wireframes and App Experience
The proposed UX follows a reward-led flow: Plan ride -> Verify ride -> Earn points -> Join contests -> Climb 
leaderboard -> Redeem rewards. The screens are designed to feel like a daily green mobility companion rather than 
a surveillance app.
