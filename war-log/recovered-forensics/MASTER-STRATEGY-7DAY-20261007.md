## RCA: why a large asset estate did not produce leads or revenue

### What the records support

1. **Assets did not become buyer-facing tests.** The repo audit catalogues 122 GitHub repositories plus 22 local artifacts and 60 deduplicated T1 survivors, but readiness is not cleanly verified. Many entries still needed deep review; planned ranking and end-to-end matrices were not found. A count of repositories is not a buyer outcome.
2. **The offer often did not fit the audience.** The September Kilo-Bridge campaign sent a job-seeker automation pitch into service-request threads. This is a confirmed mismatch in the forensic audit.
3. **Proof and commercial terms weakened trust.** A promised demo was found to contain repeated stills/terminal views instead of the claimed live run. The repo-corpus price history spans $2,500, $12,000, $15,000 tranche, and $100,000. Kilo-Bridge pricing also varied widely. A buyer could not evaluate one stable product at one stable price.
4. **Buyer intent was not handled as a funnel.** The LinkedIn record has roughly 40–50 documented premium proposals, but the audit explicitly says sent totals are unreconciled. It records zero verified paid outcomes, a small number of genuine buyer replies, and one Karan conversation that stalled after a scope-lock message. Some replies were job-seeker, supplier, or student signals rather than purchase intent.
5. **Response speed and follow-through were inconsistent.** Some proposals were sent overnight or many hours after requests; the speed rule was not operationally enforced. Warm replies and follow-ups were not consistently closed with a next step.
6. **Receipts did not reliably establish execution.** “Served” or “sent” labels sometimes lacked a destination receipt; duplicate ledger ticks inflated activity. This made it difficult to know exposure, follow-up state, and conversion.
7. **Distribution was not actually tested for several lanes.** KDP titles were live, but the audit found no verified Select promotion and little measured exposure. Product stores and freelance listings were mostly plans, not active listings. Agent School had no recorded paid enrollment and conflicting send counts.
8. **Some lanes had structural constraints.** The historical job-search lane recorded zero interviews/offers and had role/location/resume and execution issues. Its phone-screen constraint also conflicts with recruiter workflows. XTTS personal-voice cloning was marked non-commercial in the audit and should not be treated as a sales product.

### What remains a hypothesis

- The LinkedIn “looking for work” signal may confuse prospective buyers, but the recovered records do not establish that it caused the lack of sales. Profile positioning should be checked against the current live profile before editing.
- “The network is mostly freshers” is not established by these audits. Historical contact/title counts do not prove who is an active buyer or follower.
- The audits cannot show that the market rejected the assets or the underlying books. Several offers never received a clean, measured test with suitable buyers and valid proof.
- September snapshots may not describe the live state on October 7. Recheck the current profile, inbox, offers, demo, listings, and payment ledger before acting.

### Root cause in one sentence

**The system produced plans, assets, and activity, but did not consistently complete the chain from a current qualified buyer, to a matching offer and truthful proof, to a clear next step, to verified payment and delivery.**

## Funnel ledger and definitions

Use one row per person/request, not per automation tick:

`source | request date | buyer role | stated need | fit (yes/no/unknown) | offer/version | price | send date/time | destination receipt | reply | objection | next action/date | deposit evidence | delivery evidence | collected amount`

- **Qualified lead:** identifiable buyer with a current, specific need that the offer can meet.
- **Sent:** visible destination evidence exists; a queued or served instruction does not count.
- **Reply:** buyer’s own response is captured; automated detections and our outgoing messages do not count.
- **Proposal:** one written scope and price actually received by the buyer.
- **Won:** deposit or payment is independently visible in the payment record.
- **Delivered:** buyer-facing deliverable and acceptance/receipt are saved.
- **Revenue:** collected funds only. Do not count proposal value, expected royalties, or an outgoing payment message.

### Seven-day scorecard

These are operating targets, not promised outcomes:

- 10 or fewer genuinely qualified prospects reviewed.
- Up to 10 individually matched offers, only if that many qualified opportunities exist.
- 100% of claimed sends have destination receipts.
- 100% of substantive replies have a dated next action.
- At least one written scope conversation sought.
- **North-star outcome:** one verified deposit. A result of zero deposits is still useful only if the evidence identifies the failed stage.

Do not use “reply rate” or “close rate” until numerator and denominator are receipt-clean.

## Operating rules for this experiment

1. One buyer segment, one offer, one pilot price, one proof asset, one next step.
2. Match the offer to a need the buyer actually stated. Do not infer buyer intent from a job-seeker request, a profile view, or a connection count.
3. Keep job seeking and service selling in separate funnels. Check the live profile before changing its positioning; the audits do not prove that one headline change will create leads.
4. Make no “production-ready,” “live,” “working,” or “100+ validated” claim without matching current evidence.
5. No broad outreach batch until the first five to ten qualified offers are reconciled and reviewed.
6. Do not treat a declined buyer, job-seeker, supplier, or student as a service lead.
7. Preserve written-first selling if that is the operating constraint; make the demo, scope, and next step clear enough to reduce trust friction without promising a live call.
8. A payment request is not payment. A payment receipt is not delivery. Keep both receipts.

## Outcome range and interpretation

The recovered audits support a **low and uncertain seven-day cash range—₹0 to a few thousand rupees** for the existing service-page/Kilo-Bridge path, and zero near-term cash for most other lanes as then documented. The repo audit’s larger ticket sizes are not realistic seven-day expectations while proof and pricing remain unresolved. This plan improves the quality of the test; it cannot guarantee a buyer or deposit.

If ten qualified, timely, receipt-verified offers produce no substantive buyer response, revise the audience or proof before raising volume. If buyers reply but do not progress, inspect scope clarity and trust. If a buyer accepts scope but does not pay, inspect price/payment friction. If paid work cannot be delivered reproducibly, stop selling that scope until delivery is fixed.
