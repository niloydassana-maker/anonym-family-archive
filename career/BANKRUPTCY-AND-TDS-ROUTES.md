# SECTION A — PERSONAL INSOLVENCY / BANKRUPTCY (HONEST)

## A1. IBC 2016 Sec 94+ Fresh Start — does ₹50L qualify? **NO. Plainly: no.**

- The Fresh Start Process (IBC Part III, Sec 94–187) was designed for the poorest micro-debtors. Thresholds under the IBBI (Application by Fresh Start Debtor) Regulations, 2017, as verifiably notified:
  - Defaulted debt: **₹35,000 maximum** (the ₹60,000 figure often quoted is the *annual income* cap, not the debt cap — the debt cap was ₹35,000; no verified formal notification raising it to fit ₹50L has been found as of today).
  - Total income in the 12-month qualifying period: **₹60,000 maximum**.
  - Aggregate assets: **₹20,000 maximum**.
- **₹50,00,000 of debt is ~143x the debt cap.** Fresh start is arithmetically impossible, not merely unlikely. If any notification raised the cap, it would still be in the lakhs range, not crores — do not wait for it.
- Second, deeper problem: **IBC individual insolvency is not even operational for ordinary individuals.** The Central Government notified Part III only for **personal guarantors to corporate debtors** (notification of 15-Nov-2019). A standalone individual debtor (Kunal's situation) cannot access IBC insolvency resolution or bankruptcy today. The fresh start door is closed twice over.
- Honest conclusion: **IBC route = dead end for this case. The working statute for personal bankruptcy is the Provincial Insolvency Act 1920 (A2).**

## A2. Provincial Insolvency Act 1920 — the live statutory bankruptcy route

- **Forum:** "District Court" as defined in the Act. Kunal is in Kolkata — the High Court of Calcutta (Original Side) / City Civil Court complex has jurisdiction; file where the advocate advises after checking current practice. He must have resided or carried on business in the jurisdiction for the required period.
- **No debt ceiling.** Unlike IBC fresh start, PIA 1920 imposes **no upper limit on debt size** — ₹50L can go through this route. Creditor petitions need ≥ ₹500; a debtor's own petition needs proof of an **"act of insolvency" within 3 months preceding the petition** (e.g., suspension of payment, transfer with intent to defeat creditors, fraudulent preference). With zero income for 6 months and stopped EMIs, "suspension of payment" is evidencable (bounced NACH/EMI records, bank statements, written communication to banks saying he cannot pay).
- **Process:**
  1. Advocate drafts insolvency petition + affidavit of assets/liabilities (week 1–2).
  2. Court may appoint an **Interim Receiver** immediately on presentation.
  3. **Adjudication order** — typically within weeks of admission. Debtor becomes "insolvent"; **all divisible property vests in the Receiver/Official Assignee** (bank balances, receivables, movables, anything in his name).
  4. **Asset surrender is mandatory.** Retained only: wearing apparel, tools of trade, and limited statutory exemptions. Property in Father's name is not touched — property in Kunal's name is.
  5. **Discharge** (Sec 41/43): usually granted 1–3 years in, sometimes suspended until assets are realized. Discharge releases him from provable debts — **but NOT from:**
     - debts/liabilities due to the **Government (Crown debts — income tax dues survive)**,
     - liabilities founded on fraud / fraudulent breach of trust,
     - fines and penalties,
     - **secured debts** — the secured creditor realizes its security independently (discharge only kills the personal liability for shortfall in some cases — verify per loan with the advocate).
- **Cost:** court fee nominal (hundreds of rupees); realistic all-in with advocate, receiver commission (1–5% of assets realized), incidentals: **₹40,000 – ₹1,50,000 over the life of the proceeding.**
- **Timeline:** adjudication in weeks; full discharge typically **1–3 years**; court records are public and permanent.
- **Credit death:** insolvency adjudication + discharge sit on CIBIL/bureau records effectively **7+ years post-closure** and permanently in public court records. This is the nuclear option.

## A3. Practical reality — OTS/restructuring is the 80% path; bankruptcy is the 20% nuclear option

- **OTS (One-Time Settlement):** every bank has a board-approved OTS policy for stressed retail borrowers. Market-typical settlement of unsecured personal loans for genuinely distressed borrowers: **40–70% of outstanding**, usually lump-sum or 1–3 month installments. Rules of engagement:
  - Everything **in writing**. Settlement letter BEFORE paying a rupee. NOC + closure letter after final payment.
  - Ensure bureau status updates to **"Settled/Closed" — never "Written-off"** (still damaging, but 7-year clock and materially better).
- **RBI restructuring:** there is **no RBI statutory scheme for personal-loan restructuring** (the COVID Resolution Framework 2.0 lapsed). Restructuring = tenure extension / EMI reduction / moratorium under the lender's board-approved policy and RBI's Prudential Framework for Resolution of Stressed Assets (07-Jun-2019). Always written, always before default deepens.
- **Why bankruptcy is last for this debt mix (~₹50L unsecured/secured):**
  - **Secured loans:** insolvency does NOT save the collateral. Bank proceeds under **SARFAESI** (secured debt ≥ ₹1L: 60-day Sec 13(2) notice → possession without court) and/or **DRT** (claims ≥ ₹20L under RDDBFI). Bankruptcy adds nothing on the secured side; OTS/restructuring or asset sale does.
  - **Credit death 3–7+ years** vs "settled" status recovering in ~3 years.
  - **Government/tax dues survive discharge** — bankruptcy does not solve income-tax exposure if any exists.
  - **Criminal exposure unaffected:** cheque/NACH bounce cases u/s 138 NI Act proceed regardless; OTS actually defuses them.
  - Cost-benefit: PIA costs ₹40k–1.5L + 1–3 years + permanent record, to extinguish mainly *unsecured* personal liability. OTS at 40–70% on the unsecured portion may cost less and preserves the credit record partially.
- **Harassment protection (use now):** RBI recovery-agent norms — calls only 8am–7pm, no intimidation/coercion, agents must carry authorization. Escalation: bank grievance cell → nodal officer → **RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in** (30-day rule). Written-only communication (A4).

## 1. Sec 80U — self disability (Father)

- **Schizophrenia qualifies as a mental-illness disability under the RPwD Act 2016** (Sec 80U read with the Act's recognized conditions — mental illness is expressly included).
- Deduction amounts (flat, regardless of actual spend):
  - **₹75,000** — disability certified **40–79%**
  - **₹1,25,000** — certified **80%+ (severe)**
- **Certificate requirement:** issued by a **District Medical Board** (medical board constituted under the RPwD Act). A **psychiatrist sits on the board** — the diagnosis itself is examinable there.
- **Retroactive certification is allowed:** if the condition existed in the relevant year but the certificate was obtained later, the deduction can still be claimed for that year. Get the paperwork moving now; the law does not punish late certification.

## 2. Sec 80DD — dependent with disability (Mother)

- **Dependent with disability deduction:** for a dependent (spouse, children, parents, siblings) with a certified disability — **Mother (stroke history; 40%+ certification needed)**.
