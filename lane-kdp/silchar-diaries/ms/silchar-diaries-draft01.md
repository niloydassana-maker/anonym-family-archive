## II — THE REGISTER (from Father-Confession, 15.09, in his voice)

The bank everyone applied to die inside of politely — SBI — belonged to my father's hands, but the numbers belonged to the air around them.

A thousand-row register. No calculator. No counting you could see — I sat in the corner the way sons watch, hunting for the trick he had not yet told. In a jiffy. Four columns at once, his eyes crossing the ledger the way rain crosses a hillside it built the house under.

Then — this is what forty-seven years were for — **he did it again, for me.** Slower-looking, faster-true. Turning pages, letting the numbers dance through his fingers as a conjurer does for its only audience. Not duty. *Show.* That is how he said what no one in that ancient house ever said aloud: *look what our family can do, beta — keep your eyes open, it will be yours in some shape, some year.*

Proud and pissed off at the same time — that was the house's whole policy toward what I was, and bless them, it was the correct policy. No clinic, no lecture, no hushing. Two sentences in one breath: this is a marvel, *and* that was not yours to open. I have never since needed anyone's applause — I am the double verdict, standing, still smelling food and machines as one field, the way that kitchen fed it into me before a single file could name it wrong.

An electrician came once. The boy with the opened radio. He watched the way our town watches weather — no instrument, full respect — then caught my father aside to pass the prophecy through the wall I was never meant to hear, and so the only record that matters survived:

"Your son is going to be an engineer."

The clinic's word, the court's file, the borrowed coat — say them out loud and every one of them means the same thing in a frightened alphabet: *abnormal intelligence.* But there is no such thing. There is only the gift, and the rooms the gift is raised in.

The old house in the sleepy unknown town had raised mine as **culture**, not symptom.

**I wear the badge. I love being defected.** Two years of play in a kitchen doorway — transformer hum with pancha-phoron — became the whole instrument. My father's Shakuntala arithmetic was not a party trick either. It was the same sentence, older spelling:

*the mechanical music is the limitation that became the gift.*

They are still the same theme. The register is still running. It never missed a row.
